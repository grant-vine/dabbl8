# Competitive synthesis

Groove OS is the main musical benchmark. It advertises eight internal tracks, shared pools of twelve FM and eight VA voices, a drum kit, performance scenes, effects and a richer arrangement workflow. Its credits identify it as proprietary. Treat its interface and demos as a functional benchmark; do not extract its firmware, sounds, code or artwork for reuse. Its feature page also describes shared FM controls and bounded project resources, so eight tracks does not mean every function is independent or unlimited. Source: https://www.groove-os.com/ and https://www.groove-os.com/features and https://www.groove-os.com/credits. No target-side comparison was performed here.

SLOOP 2.4.1 is a performance-oriented GPL fork with synth and drum sequencing, locks, microtiming, fills and quick chains. Its latest inspected commit fixes noise in imported DX7 patches with amplitude modulation sensitivity. Candidate work: expressive sequencing and performance gestures; compare FM6 changes against upstream before taking a patch. Source: https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1.

Melodee 0.12 is the strongest architectural reference. Source defines sixteen voice slots and a weighted budget of sixteen units: FM6 voices cost one, other voices two, retaining an eight-voice effective budget for those other engines. It remains four-track, adds event recording and expanded pattern/tuning resources, and retires user samples and SLICE. Candidate work: cost-aware allocation and bounded event recording. Do not interpret its sixteen slots as sixteen voices of any engine. Source: https://github.com/keremimo/melodee.

X0X specializes in classic drum machines, two acid voices and break-oriented performance. Candidate work: a carefully bounded acid engine and drum accents, not importing its complete multi-part architecture into one Dabbl8 track. Audit Open303, TB3PO, drum material and individual file licenses before reuse. Source: https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x.

Jangada emphasizes layered oscillators, evolving textures and drones. Candidate work: new presets and selected engine modes after the timing budget is established. FoMni emphasizes chord selection, strumming and rhythm performance; its musical event-generation model fits Dabbl8 better than creating a separate firmware personality. Sources: https://github.com/zednaked/jangada and https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-fomni.

FM-1+VA provides another interface and macro benchmark, but source reuse is not assumed. The stock patcher is relevant to detune/aftertouch ideas, not a code base for a replacement OS. Lunar Modulator is a preview/simulator candidate rather than a confirmed installable donor. Prioritize features demonstrated on real hardware. Sound demos cannot establish relative loudness, CPU cost or firmware stability without controlled recordings.
